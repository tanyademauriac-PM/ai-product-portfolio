01 · The Situation

A qualification failure created an urgent compliance problem.

A serious customer audit issue reached senior leadership. A worker who had installed miles of large-scale gas pipeline had been evaluated by someone who lacked the required advanced certification for that work. The worker's resulting qualification came into question, and the customer had to remove the newly installed pipeline.

The customer also needed a credible remediation plan showing how it would prevent the same type of qualification failure. Existing work was reprioritized immediately. The expectation was to deliver a responsible solution as quickly as possible.

My manager brought me into an intensive knowledge-transfer session. I had to absorb a large amount of unfamiliar business and product context very quickly and begin shaping the response while the team prepared to move.

02 · The Challenge

The product logic extended far beyond a certification setting.

The platform served operators and contractors. Contractors could work for multiple operators, sometimes using shared qualification content. Different operators could place different requirements on the same contractor and the same content.

The existing algorithm resolved company rules, evaluation rules, shared content, custom content, and shared resources through a strictest-applicable-rule model. The most restrictive requirement governed. That approach was appropriate in a federally regulated environment where an operator remained accountable for work performed on its infrastructure.

The platform already had configurable controls and complex qualification logic, but it had no workflow that prevented an evaluator from launching an evaluation when this advanced certification was required. I extended the existing configurable control model to address that new compliance risk.

03 · My Role

I held the product response together as the work moved forward.

I rapidly learned the existing algorithm, shaped the solution, defined the expected behavior, built the requirements, and led the delivery effort. I also reprioritized the backlog, coordinated the necessary handoff of other important work, and helped technical and business audiences understand how the resulting rules would behave.

I worked closely with a long-tenured executive-level business subject-matter expert, the principal engineer, architecture, UX, account managers, and business stakeholders. The sprint team included two .NET developers, one QA professional, and two SQL developers. The principal engineer and UX were shared resources.

04 · How I Approached It

I made the complicated behavior explicit.

I worked through combinations involving shared and custom content, operators and contractors, company and evaluation scope, shared contractors, conflicting requirements, certification status, expiration, and the existing rules hierarchy.

I captured the logic in workflows, flow diagrams, matrices, detailed user stories, and precise acceptance criteria. QA used my acceptance criteria to build its test scenarios. When an edge case required a product decision, I developed a recommended behavior and validated it with the appropriate business stakeholders and account managers.

The model was difficult for people across the organization to understand. I repeatedly explained it to developers, QA, business stakeholders, account managers, and leadership. I also wrote a detailed explanation of the algorithm and new controls so account managers could describe the behavior accurately to customers.

More detail on the analysis and validation

The long-tenured business subject-matter expert helped me understand how custom content, shared content, operator and contractor relationships, and existing company and evaluation rules interacted. I used that knowledge to determine whether the existing strictest-rule model could support the new requirement.

Each implementation increment was demonstrated in sprint review. The organization used weekly releases and two-week sprint reviews, so the broader product and development teams, managers, leadership, account management, and other stakeholders could see and review the evolving capability throughout delivery.

My responsibility was to determine and document the expected product behavior. QA owned the detailed test scenarios and worked directly from the acceptance criteria I provided.

05 · The Key Product Decision

Urgency changed the delivery sequence while the architecture stayed intact.

Engineering was appropriately careful about shortcuts that could create brittle architecture or technical debt. We preserved the configurable product design and changed the sequence in which users received it.

The team first created the new configuration capability in the backend. A script populated the necessary values while the team connected them to the evaluation-launch restriction. This allowed the customer to receive the critical protection much sooner. The administrative UI, additional granularity, and expiration notifications followed in usable increments.

Company-level configuration came first because it matched how the originating customer used content in the platform. That gave the team the fastest responsible path to the immediate remediation need. Evaluation-level controls followed to give other customers greater precision.

The first release used the same configurable architecture that supported the completed capability. Every sprint moved the product closer to the full experience while delivering usable value.

06 · What We Built

The completed capability combined prevention, explanation, and advance notice.

The solution included four certification controls. Customers could configure the advanced evaluator requirement at company or evaluation level and apply it separately to their own employees and contractor employees.

Evaluation-level control provided important precision because a customer might need the more stringent requirement for a specific high-risk qualification without applying it to every evaluation. When the resolved rule required the certification, an evaluator who lacked it could not launch the evaluation.

This was preventive enforcement before the evaluation began. Tooltips helped administrators understand the controls. When a launch was prevented, the message explained the requirement and directed the evaluator to the company administrator. The final increment added 90, 60, and 30-day expiration notices for the evaluator and the appropriate administrator.

Four controls Company and evaluation scope for employees and contractors
Rules resolution The strictest applicable requirement governs
Preventive enforcement An ineligible evaluator cannot launch the evaluation
Explanatory UX Administrators and evaluators understand what to do next

07 · Delivery Under Pressure

Discovery, solution design, requirements, and implementation overlapped.

I personally reprioritized the backlog and sprint work, surfaced the tradeoffs, and communicated what would move. Important work that still needed to continue was transferred to another sprint team, which required a deliberate handoff and knowledge transfer.

My primary team started on the compliance initiative immediately. I introduced the team to what we already understood, then expanded and refined the requirements, stories, and acceptance criteria week by week as implementation progressed.

The developers needed unusually explicit requirements because the rules were so complicated. I stayed methodical, made decisions as new detail surfaced, and kept the sprint team and broader organization aligned. The complete capability was delivered over approximately one quarter.

08 · Outcome

The emergency response became a durable product pattern.

The originating customer enabled the controls with help from our organization. The capability supported the remediation plan it presented after the audit issue. When a configured requirement applied, the product prevented an evaluator who lacked the certification from launching the affected evaluation.

Product data showed that additional customers enabled the settings. The functionality remains in the product as originally implemented, with no later redesign required and no known problems that required rework. The company later used this implementation as a model for additional company-level and evaluation-level controls.

The advanced certification was part of an existing paid training and recertification program. The SaaS controls strengthened the connection between the platform and that offering and enabled a broader commercial opportunity. I owned the product capability, while other teams owned the certification program, marketing, and any later commercial results.

Closing Perspective

Calm, detailed product leadership can still move very quickly.

This work required me to learn an unfamiliar high-risk problem and a complicated legacy rules model at the same time. I had to make sound decisions while implementation was already moving, keep technical and business audiences aligned, and make sure important work displaced by the emergency still had a path forward.

The solution lasted because urgency never became permission to be careless. We delivered the protection the customer needed quickly and completed a reusable platform capability that still works as originally designed.
