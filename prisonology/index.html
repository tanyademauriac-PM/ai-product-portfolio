01 · The productization gap

The service, application, and AI existed. The customer and consultant workflows still needed to become one dependable product.

Prisonology already had account, case, and report-status foundations. Its AI could read a 40–50 page PSR, extract case data, and generate a draft security designation. I focused on the connected workflow required to sell, process, review, and deliver that result responsibly.

Question I wanted to answer
How can the product preserve AI speed while making expert judgment, evidence, exceptions, and approval visible?
02 · The customer journey

One core journey supports legal-team and self-submission paths.

I treated legal teams as the primary buyer and case owner while preserving a direct path for an individual defendant. The branch at case creation handles authorization and sharing rules inside the same product.

03 · Users & responsibilities

I separated who buys, who the case is about, and who owns the release decision.

These working proto-personas are based on the service and challenge constraints. They define workflow responsibilities and make no claim to research-backed biographies or demographics.

Primary customer

Legal team

Submits for a client, manages payment and access, and needs a fast, defensible result for client strategy.

Secondary customer

Individual defendant

May submit for themselves and needs clear guidance, privacy, and appropriate sharing controls.

Expert analyst & approver

Prisonology consultant

Verifies source evidence, resolves ambiguity, adds placement analysis, and approves the report for release.

04 · The AI + expert operating model

The consultant's work is a core product experience.

Unresolved critical fields, conflicts, missing information, or incomplete placement analysis block release. The system preserves the evidence and decision history.

Platform + AI

Extract and draft

Read the PSR, extract fields, calculate the draft designation, run checks, and route flags to review.

Consultant

Verify and analyze

Compare critical fields with source pages, record overrides, request information, and add placement analysis.

Release gate

Approve and lock

Confirm every consequential item is resolved, record the approver and model version, then release securely.

06 · Proposed MVP decisions

I designed the MVP as a complete paid service loop.

The first release covers discovery through secure delivery and includes the controls required for high-stakes, sensitive information.

01

Use one customer flow

Support legal-team and self-submission branches through case ownership and permissions.

02

Validate before payment

Confirm that the PSR is readable and processable before charging the customer.

03

Require expert approval

Record approval on every customer-visible report and block unresolved critical issues.

04

Keep delivery secure

Use plain status language and email alerts while keeping report content inside the authenticated portal.

05

Preserve the record

Retain evidence links, overrides, receipts, model and report versions, and activity history.

07 · Validation plan

Test both customer paths, benchmark AI + expert review, then pilot the closed-loop service.

Customer evidence

Review current order data and interview both customer types to validate who buys and how cases are managed.

Operational evidence

Shadow current cases, inspect existing deliverables, and time representative PSRs by complexity.

Usability + governance

Test customer and consultant flows and confirm authorization, sharing, retention, and security requirements.

The takeaway

Productizing the AI required a trustworthy service workflow around it.

I used the challenge to connect customer value, expert operations, AI quality, payment, exceptions, security, and measurement. The recommendation uses AI for speed and leaves interpretation, placement analysis, and release with the people who hold the domain expertise.
