Case Study 07 · Career pattern

Product Leadership in High-Growth Environments

High growth has been the pattern, not the exception.

Across my career, I have repeatedly joined companies, products, or product organizations at expansion points — when the existing way of working was no longer enough for what came next.

01 · The Recruiter Version

If a role asks for high-growth experience, this is the evidence.

My experience is not concentrated in one type of high-growth company. I have worked through several forms of expansion: an early company building its product function, a new product going from idea to MVP, an enterprise organization scaling dramatically, and a product organization maturing while supporting a complex flagship platform.

02 · Four Expansion Points

The common thread was joining when the next stage had to be built.

The specific challenge changed, but the environment was familiar: growth created new demands, the existing model had limits, and the organization needed clearer product leadership to move forward.

Cognistix

From small company to scalable product capability

At entry
Roughly employee #8 in an early enterprise SaaS company.
Growth context
The company was adding customers, people, and product capability while supporting a large distributed retail workforce.
My role in the next stage
I helped establish product practices, identified mobile as the next major product opportunity, championed it for more than two years, and led the product from concept through launch.
Scale evidence
Cognistix Mobile became a net-new product used by tens of thousands of field workers.
Raytheon

From concentrated delivery to enterprise complexity

At entry
A comparatively small organization with concentrated customer scope.
Growth context
The surrounding organization expanded from roughly 50 to 500 people and broadened into more global and cross-industry work.
My role in the next stage
I worked across stakeholders, systems, requirements, integrations, data flows, and solution design as the operating environment became more complex.
Scale evidence
My work included GM's U.S. learning environment serving more than one million users.
Enroute

From idea to 0→1 SaaS product

At entry
A business opportunity without a defined product.
Growth context
The company needed to turn a concept into something customers could understand, evaluate, and eventually buy.
My role in the next stage
I led discovery, product strategy, roadmap development, workflow definition, UX collaboration, and MVP planning.
Scale evidence
The work produced a defined MVP and an initial prospective customer in the Los Angeles chauffeuring market.
Energy Worldnet

From project orientation to stronger product scale

At entry
A regulated SaaS company building a stronger Product organization.
Growth context
The company was evolving its product operating model while continuing to expand a complex enterprise platform and commercial product capabilities.
My role in the next stage
I owned strategy, roadmap, discovery, prioritization, delivery, and launch readiness for the flagship platform across three sprint teams.
Scale evidence
Weekly releases, multiple concurrent strategic initiatives, a net-new OJT subscription product, and faster discovery and product documentation cycles.
03 · What High Growth Required From Me

High growth changes the job before it changes the org chart.

In these environments, I was rarely stepping into a mature, perfectly defined product machine. The work required operating while structure was still emerging and helping build the structure the next stage needed.

01Create clarity from ambiguity

Define the problem, product direction, MVP, or operating structure before there is a settled playbook.

02Build while the environment is changing

Keep delivery moving while teams, processes, products, or customer expectations are evolving.

03Prioritize under real constraints

Make tradeoffs across limited capacity, multiple stakeholders, and competing strategic needs.

04Add structure without adding drag

Create enough process and decision clarity to support growth without slowing the organization down.

05Scale across people and systems

Work effectively across more users, teams, integrations, dependencies, and operational complexity.

06See what the next stage needs

Recognize capabilities the organization will need before they become obvious blockers.

04 · The Takeaway

I do not just have exposure to high growth. It has been the recurring environment of my career.

I have worked in early companies, 0→1 product creation, rapidly expanding enterprise organizations, and product teams maturing how they operate.

Those experiences taught me to be comfortable before everything is defined, to create structure without waiting for perfect conditions, and to help teams move into a bigger operating model while still delivering.

When a company is entering its next stage, that environment feels familiar to me.
