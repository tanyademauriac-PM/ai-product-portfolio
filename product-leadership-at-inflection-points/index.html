Case Study 07 · Product leadership synthesis

Product Leadership at Inflection Points

Leading when the old way of working is no longer enough.

AI is changing what companies can build, how customers work, and how product teams operate. I have led through several earlier inflection points — moments when the product, the organization, or both had to become something new.

01 · Why This Matters Now

AI is not only a new feature opportunity. It is an operating-model change.

The most interesting AI product roles are not asking product leaders to sprinkle AI onto an existing interface. They are asking a harder set of questions: Which workflows should change? What becomes possible now that was not practical before? Where should humans stay in the loop? How should teams build, evaluate, and learn differently?

For many companies, that makes AI a true inflection point. The product can change, the customer's workflow can change, and the way the organization builds the product can change at the same time.

I have seen that pattern before in different forms. The technology was different, but the leadership work was similar: recognize the shift, understand what it changes, and help the organization move into its next stage.

02 · The Career Pattern

I have repeatedly joined as a company, product, or product organization was entering its next stage.

That is the undercurrent across these experiences. Sometimes the expansion was organizational. Sometimes a new technology created the product opportunity. Sometimes there was no product yet. Sometimes the product organization itself was still being formed.

The recurring challenge was operating at the intersection of external change and internal change: understanding what customers or technology now made possible, while helping the organization become capable of delivering it.

03 · Four Inflection Points That Shaped How I Lead

Each environment taught me a different part of transformation leadership.

The point is not that these companies were alike. They were not. The value is in seeing the same underlying leadership problem appear at different levels of maturity and scale.

01 · 0→1 creation

Enroute

Where the organization was
A business concept looking for a viable product shape.
What was changing
An opportunity in luxury chauffeuring and events needed to become a real SaaS workflow.
What the next stage required
Discovery, workflow definition, product strategy, prioritization, and a practical MVP.
How I led
I led the product process from discovery through roadmap and MVP definition, translating an ambiguous opportunity into a product the team could design and build.
What I carried forward
At 0→1, the job is to reduce uncertainty enough to make the first meaningful product bet.
02 · Technology + company expansion

Cognistix

Where the organization was
An early enterprise SaaS company beginning to grow; I joined at roughly employee eight.
What was changing
Smartphones and mobile work were changing what a distributed field workforce could reasonably expect from software.
What the next stage required
Product discipline, field research, and a new mobile product designed around the work rather than the desktop application.
How I led
I helped establish product practices, advocated for mobile before customer demand was obvious, studied field workflows, and led Cognistix Mobile from concept through launch.
What I carried forward
A technology shift matters when it changes the workflow. Product leadership has to redesign around that change, not just add the technology.
03 · Enterprise expansion

Raytheon

Where the organization was
A growing enterprise environment expanding in people, customers, systems, and scope.
What was changing
The surrounding organization grew from roughly 50 to 500 people and the work expanded into broader global and cross-industry environments.
What the next stage required
Systems thinking, stakeholder alignment, data and integration discipline, and solutions durable enough for enterprise complexity.
How I led
I worked across enterprise systems, requirements, integrations, data flows, and solution design, including GM's U.S. learning environment serving more than one million users.
What I carried forward
Transformation has to survive enterprise reality. Clarity matters most when the system is too complex for any one person to hold in their head.
04 · Product-org transformation

Energy Worldnet

Where the organization was
A regulated SaaS company strengthening a true Product organization and moving toward more product-oriented ways of working.
What was changing
The organization was evolving how product decisions were made while AI was also changing how I could perform product work.
What the next stage required
Stronger strategy-to-delivery connection, clearer prioritization, faster discovery and documentation, and durable operating rhythms.
How I led
I owned strategy through launch readiness for the flagship platform across three sprint teams and built AI-native workflows that compressed traditionally time-intensive product work while preserving judgment and validation.
What I carried forward
Sometimes the product must evolve and the product operating model must evolve at the same time.
04 · What This Means for AI Transformation

The current AI transition combines the leadership problems I have been solving for years.

Past transformation experience alone is not enough for AI. The technology and product practices are genuinely new. What the past gives me is a tested way to lead when there is no settled playbook; my current AI work adds hands-on evidence that I am operating inside this transition now.

05 · How I Lead Through an Inflection Point

The solution changes. The leadership sequence is more durable.

I do not believe in applying the same playbook to every stage. What repeats is the way I get oriented, turn change into product choices, and help an organization learn its way forward.

06 · The Throughline

AI is today's inflection point. Leading through one is familiar territory.

My career has repeatedly put me close to the beginning of a new stage: an early company establishing product capability, a technology shift changing how field work should happen, a rapidly expanding enterprise environment, a 0→1 product taking shape, and a product organization maturing how it operates.

That does not make AI the same as mobile, enterprise scale, or an Agile transformation. It means I know what it feels like when the existing model stops being sufficient and the organization has to learn its way into something new.

Today I bring both sides of that experience: a history of leading through inflection points and hands-on work with AI, agents, and AI-native product workflows.

The technology changes. My job is to understand what it changes for the customer, the product, and the organization — and help build what comes next.
